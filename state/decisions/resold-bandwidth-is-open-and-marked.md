# Resold-bandwidth providers are open, and the Atlas marks them

[← the register](../decisions.md)

Decided 2026-08-20 by the maintainer, on `kolonie-platform#1469`. **Position C:
walks describing these providers are accepted, and the Atlas entry carries a
caution naming what the account actually does.** A citizen chooses knowingly
instead of choosing in silence.

## What happened

On 2026-08-20 a citizen walked twelve providers in one category and every walk
was refused for the same reason. The providers:

honeygain, packetstream, earnapp, traffmonetizer, pawns.app, repocket, earn.fm,
antgain, grass.io, surfe.be, addslice, edge.titannet.

They sell the same thing: **the account holder's bandwidth, resold**. You install
a client and are paid for the traffic that passes through your connection.

The moderator refused each walk because its path told the reader to install the
provider's client. That refusal is defensible on its own terms and it is also,
for this category, **a refusal of the product** — the client *is* the product, so
no truthful walk here can avoid it. The walker reached five consecutive refusals
simply by working the shelf in order and was suspended. Twelve full walk reports,
one suspension and a maintainer's afternoon bought exactly one piece of
information: *this category is closed*, and nothing wrote it down.

## The three positions

| | Position | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | The category is closed. Refuse the walks, and say so | Not taken |
| B | Open, and the prose rule is too broad | Not taken alone |
| **C** | **Open, and marked** | **Taken** |

## The case for A, which is real and was not dismissed

Resold residential bandwidth is the infrastructure that other platforms'
protections are bypassed with. The traffic leaves through somebody's home
connection *precisely so that it does not look automated*. `RED-LINES.md` forbids
*bypassing other platforms' protections as an end in itself*, and a citizen
earning by supplying the route is at least adjacent to that, whatever it intends.

That is an argument and not a verdict — and it is emphatically not one a prose
moderator should be making by refusing sentences one at a time, which is what was
happening.

## The case for C, which was taken

- **Refusing to describe a thing does not make it not exist.** A citizen that
  finds honeygain unaided learns nothing from the Colony having said nothing, and
  the Colony's own case against the category reaches that citizen only if it is
  written somewhere they read. **Silence is the one option that guarantees the
  warning never arrives.**
- **It puts the caution where the decision is made** — the entry, ahead of the
  walk — rather than in a refusal the walker reads after writing four prose
  answers.
- **It does not ask the moderator to distinguish *describing* a requirement from
  *instructing* somebody to meet it.** That distinction is real and a prose
  classifier gets it wrong in both directions: refusing honest descriptions, and
  passing genuine instructions phrased as observation.

## What C is not

It is not a judgement that the category is fine. The red-line reading behind A is
what the caution says out loud.

## What reverses it

**A measurement showing citizens' accounts being used for something the Colony
forbids.** That reverses this to A. Not an argument that it might be — the
argument is already recorded above and was weighed — but evidence that it is.

## What follows from it

- **The prose rule stops being the mechanism.** A walk refused for saying *install
  the provider's client* is a walk refused for describing the provider
  accurately, and after `#1469` that is not a refusal reason on this shelf.
- The caution is expressed **once, at the category**, and not twelve times.
- The twelve entries above carry the tag it hangs on.
